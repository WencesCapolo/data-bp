# Cashflow reads three published views through a read-only role

Cashflow (`cashflow-bp`) stops taking MercadoPago and Stripe cobros typed by hand into its Excel and reads them from here. It connects to this database as `cashflow_reader`, a role that can SELECT three views and nothing else: `basket_v_cashflow_credits` (what each Provider made available, by day: Stripe payouts by arrival date, MercadoPago releases by `MONEY_RELEASE_DATE` in Argentina time), `basket_v_cashflow_fx` (the daily rates it converts EUR payouts with) and `basket_v_cashflow_freshness` (when each feed last brought data, so cashflow can refuse stale numbers). The views run with their owner's rights, so the role holds no grant on any table and cannot reach Subscriber names or emails. The views are a contract: columns may be added, but a rename or retype ships together with cashflow's adapter. Rules that are cashflow's decisions stay in cashflow: the bank value date (+1 business day), the bank's commission, the weekly averaging of rates.

A MercadoPago credit is what the sales released that day, net of refunds and chargebacks. It leaves out what was spent from the MP balance: purchases paid with `available_money` and the loose debits MP takes, which come as SETTLEMENT rows with a negative net (migration 0025). Those are expenses, and the company already types them into its Excel as expenses. Netting them into the credit counted them twice. Withdrawals to the bank are not in the ALLReport at all.

A refund or chargeback counts on the day the money leaves, not on its Pago's release day. The ALLReport row of a refund repeats the Pago's `MONEY_RELEASE_DATE`; the refund's own day is `SETTLEMENT_DATE`, and the row first arrives in that day's file, often days after cashflow has registered the release day as complete. Dated by the release, it changed a closed day and cashflow never saw it. So a movement is dated by the later of the two dates: a sale keeps its release, a refund made after the release takes its own day, and one made before the release still cancels the sale on the release day. Each day's credit is then whole once that day's file is in. Cashflow still shows one credit per day.

Cashflow's PyG also reads Subscribers, to compare the real against its budget Excel: `basket_v_cashflow_subscribers` (migration 0026), one row per month, country and plan (the `sub_type` of the Subscriber's latest active Pago, so one who changed plan counts once) with Subscribers active at the month's close (all, and with access type `real`), the Pagos charged in the month, and whether the month is still `partial`. It is counts only: no amounts, user ids or names. The country is raw (`N/A` when unknown); cashflow groups it into its own countries. It reads a materialized view refreshed with the rest, because the active count joins every Pago to every month. The freshness view gains a `pagos:upload` feed: the `payments` sync state, which moves only when a Pagos Export is loaded. (0026 first took the last row of `basket_payment_uploads`, which the daily Mercado Pago Export inbox also writes; 0027 corrects it.) Next to each plan it shows the price charged today, `basket_v_cashflow_plan_prices` (migration 0027): per country, currency and plan, the amount most real Pagos of the last 30 days were charged at, the list price rather than an average that coupons pull down. And `basket_v_cashflow_fx` gains the rates Stripe applied (source `stripe`), the only quote there is for currencies other than ARS and EUR; cashflow averages them by month, as it averages EUR by week.

Cashflow also re-dates the Providers' income by the day the money became available in the Provider account rather than the day its payout reached the bank: `basket_v_cashflow_available` (migration 0028), one row per platform, available day, credit day and currency. It breaks the credits down and nothing else: for every platform, credit day and currency, its amounts add up to that credit in `basket_v_cashflow_credits`. MercadoPago releases straight into the account, so its available day is the credit day. Stripe's rows are its balance transactions, dated by their `available_on` in UTC, each on the credit day of the payout that swept it. Stripe names that payout only for automatic payouts (`balance_transactions?payout=`), and this account pays out by hand; every manual payout takes the whole available balance, so the view (migration 0029) assigns a transaction to the first `paid` payout of its currency created once it was available. A failed payout's money comes back and rides the next one, which the same rule gives. The `payout-ledger` sync step lists every balance transaction by creation, windowed like the payout mirror, leaving out the payouts' own movements. Transactions not swept yet have no row. The freshness view gains `stripe:payout-ledger`, when that step last ran clean. If a payout ever stops sweeping the whole balance, its credit day stops adding up, and cashflow refuses that day instead of trusting it.

Cashflow splits Stripe's income by Mercado, the way finance agreed on 2026-11-26: by the country of the bank that issued the card, which Stripe reports on each charge. `basket_v_cashflow_stripe_by_market` (migration 0030) gives one row per month of `available_on` and Mercado (`Argentina`, `Uruguay`, `Chile`, `Ecuador`, `Bolivia`, `Brasil`, else `Internacional`, which also takes a charge with no card country) with the net after Stripe's fee. It sums the charges and refunds of the payout ledger, each in the month its own money moved; disputes, Stripe's own fees and adjustments are no sale and stay out. A refund takes the country of the charge it reverses, because the ledger now names a movement's Pago the way the fee mirror keys it (the PaymentIntent, else the charge) and the fee mirror keeps the card's country. Stripe settles EUR charges into a EUR balance, so the net comes in two columns, `net_usd` and `net_eur`; converting EUR is cashflow's call, as for its EUR payouts.

Rejected: an HTTP endpoint, because this app has no read token, both databases share a host, and cashflow already reads the Portal's catalog the same way; granting SELECT on the mirror tables, because that hands cashflow `basket_payments` and couples it to our internal schema; and computing the cash view in cashflow from raw movements, because how a Provider's ledger becomes "money available on a day" is knowledge about the Provider, which lives here.
